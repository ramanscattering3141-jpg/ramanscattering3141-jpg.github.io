"Build a Comprehensive ECG & Cardiovascular Electrophysiology Education App"

**1. Core Product Concept.** Build a cardiovascular education application centered on ECG interpretation, cardiac electrophysiology, physiology, pathophysiology, rhythm diagnosis, and management. This must NOT be an app primarily about atrial fibrillation, Brugada syndrome, or a small collection of ECG diagnoses. It should cover the full range of ECG findings and cardiac rhythms that an internal medicine, emergency medicine, cardiology, ICU, or ACLS learner commonly encounters, with deeper coverage of important but less common conditions. The central educational principle is: **"Do not merely teach what an ECG looks like. Teach why it looks that way."** Every important ECG finding should connect: cardiac electrophysiology → conduction pathway → activation/repolarization → electrical vector → lead → waveform → ECG diagnosis → clinical significance → management. The application should function simultaneously as: ECG textbook; electrophysiology simulator; ECG morphology laboratory; rhythm simulator; ECG interpretation trainer; cardiovascular physiology teaching platform; clinical management reference; ACLS reference; randomized ECG case generator.

**2. SOURCE AND EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS.** Medical accuracy is a primary requirement. The AI must use authoritative online sources when creating clinical content. Prioritize: 1 ACC/AHA/HRS guidelines; 2 ESC guidelines; 3 AHA ACLS/CPR guidelines; 4 Canadian Cardiovascular Society guidance where applicable; 5 peer-reviewed primary literature; 6 high-quality electrophysiology and cardiology reviews. For ECG physiology, use authoritative medical/electrophysiology sources and peer-reviewed literature. The app should maintain a source record for every clinically consequential module. Each source record should contain: organization/authors; title; publication year; journal or organization; DOI or official URL; date last verified. **"Do not invent citations. Do not invent trials. Do not invent guideline recommendations. Do not assume that an old guideline is current when a newer guideline exists."** The initial clinical framework should explicitly incorporate the ACC/AHA/HRS SVT guideline (dedicated sections for AVNRT, AVRT/accessory pathways, focal AT, MAT, atrial flutter). Use the current AHA 2025 ACLS algorithms for the ACLS section, including tachycardia with a pulse and bradycardia with a pulse. Use contemporary ventricular-arrhythmia guidance for VT, VF, long-QT, Brugada, early repolarization, and other ventricular/electrical diseases. Use the current ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS AF guideline for AF management rather than relying on the older SVT guideline for AF.

**3. MAIN APP STRUCTURE** (main navigation): A. ECG Fundamentals; B. ECG Physiology; C. ECG Simulator; D. Rhythm Library; E. Conduction Disorders; F. Structural/Physiological ECG Changes; G. Ischemia & Infarction; H. Electrolytes & Toxicology; I. Inherited/Electrical Syndromes; J. Arrhythmia Management; K. ACLS ECG Reference; L. ECG Cases; M. ECG Challenge Mode; N. Physiology Sandbox.

**4. ECG FUNDAMENTALS.** Teach "What is an ECG?": membrane potentials; cardiac action potentials; depolarization; repolarization; resting membrane potential; ion gradients; electrical conduction; surface voltage; electrical vectors; electrodes; leads. 12-lead ECG: lead I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1–V6 — "Explain exactly what each lead is viewing." **"Include an interactive 3D/2D cardiac vector visualization. The user should be able to rotate the activation vector and see which leads become positive or negative."**

**5. PHYSIOLOGY-FIRST ECG ENGINE.** The application must have a physiological model underlying the ECG. **"Do NOT simply swap between pre-rendered ECG images."** The simulator should model, at an appropriate educational level: sinus node automaticity; atrial activation; AV nodal delay; AV nodal refractoriness; His bundle conduction; right bundle conduction; left bundle conduction; fascicular conduction; Purkinje activation; ventricular activation; ventricular repolarization; ectopic automaticity; re-entry; conduction velocity; refractory periods. Changing these variables should alter the ECG.

**6. THE MOST IMPORTANT INTERACTIVE FEATURE: "WHY?"** Every major ECG abnormality should have "WHY DOES IT LOOK LIKE THIS?" — when selected, show the causal chain. Example Wide QRS — Normal: His-Purkinje system → rapid synchronized ventricular activation → narrow QRS. Abnormal: ventricular origin / bundle branch block / slowed ventricular conduction → slower or altered activation → prolonged depolarization → wide QRS. "Show the mechanism visually. Apply the same principle to every diagnosis."

**7. RHYTHM LIBRARY** (comprehensive). Sinus: NSR; sinus bradycardia; sinus tachycardia; sinus arrhythmia; sinus pause; sinus arrest; sinoatrial exit block. Premature beats: PAC; PVC; interpolated PVC; aberrantly conducted PAC; compensatory vs noncompensatory pauses. Atrial tachyarrhythmias: focal AT; MAT; atrial flutter; AF; inappropriate sinus tachycardia. Junctional: junctional escape; accelerated junctional rhythm; junctional tachycardia. Ventricular: ventricular escape; AIVR; monomorphic VT; polymorphic VT; torsades; ventricular flutter; VF.

**8. AVNRT** — major dedicated module. Teach dual AV nodal physiology; fast pathway; slow pathway; differential conduction; re-entry; initiation; termination. **Animate the circuit**: atrial impulse → slow pathway → fast pathway → re-entry → rapid ventricular activation; then generate the ECG. Explain regular narrow-complex tachycardia; typical retrograde P-wave behaviour; pseudo-R′ in V1; pseudo-S in inferior leads; short RP. Allow the user to manipulate slow-pathway conduction, fast-pathway conduction, refractory periods, circuit timing, then demonstrate why the ECG changes. Management: vagal manoeuvres; adenosine; AV nodal blockers; synchronized cardioversion when appropriate; catheter ablation — **connected directly to the mechanism** (e.g. "Adenosine terminates AVNRT because the re-entry circuit depends on AV nodal tissue." "Do not teach this as memorization.") The ACC/AHA/HRS guideline provides acute and ongoing management sections for AVNRT.

**9. AVRT** — separate major module. Accessory pathway; AV node; re-entry circuit; orthodromic; antidromic; concealed APs; manifest APs. "Show the circuit dynamically." Orthodromic: atrium → AV node → His-Purkinje → ventricle → AP → atrium; show why QRS usually narrow. Antidromic: atrium → AP → ventricle → AV node/His-Purkinje → atrium; show why QRS wide. WPW/pre-excitation: short PR; delta wave; widened QRS; fusion; localization concepts. "The user should be able to change accessory pathway location and see morphology change."

**10. PRE-EXCITED AF** — dedicated module separate from ordinary AF. Show AF → chaotic atrial impulses → AP bypasses AV node → extremely rapid ventricular activation. Explain why fundamentally different from ordinary AF; show why AV nodal blockade can be problematic; connect to treatment decisions.

**11. ATRIAL FLUTTER.** Macro-reentry; typical CTI-dependent flutter; atrial rate; AV conduction ratios; 2:1; 3:1; variable. Simulate 2:1 → 3:1 → variable block; show why ventricular rate changes. "Explain sawtooth flutter waves using vector orientation."

**12. AF.** Chaotic atrial activation; multiple wavefronts/re-entry concepts; atrial refractoriness; AV nodal filtering; irregular ventricular response. Explain absent organized P waves; fibrillatory baseline; irregularly irregular rhythm. Then teach rate control; rhythm control; cardioversion; anticoagulation; stroke prevention; ablation — current AF guideline as source.

**13. AV BLOCK** — extensive module. 1st degree: increased AV nodal/His-Purkinje delay → prolonged PR. Mobitz I/Wenckebach: **animate progressive conduction delay followed by a dropped QRS**; explain mechanism. Mobitz II: constant conduction time → sudden failure. High-grade: multiple consecutive nonconducted P waves. CHB: atrial and ventricular activity completely dissociated. Then allow learner to select junctional escape vs ventricular escape and show why QRS morphology differs.

**14. BUNDLE BRANCH AND FASCICULAR BLOCKS** — dedicated modules: RBBB; LBBB; LAFB; LPFB; bifascicular; nonspecific IVCD. For each: 1 normal activation; 2 what pathway is blocked; 3 how activation reroutes; 4 how the QRS vector changes; 5 why QRS widens; 6 why particular leads become positive/negative; 7 clinical significance.

**15. ECTOPIC RHYTHMS.** Teach pacemaker hierarchy: SA node → atrial tissue → AV junction → His-Purkinje → ventricular myocardium. Allow the user to disable higher-level pacemakers and observe emergence of escape rhythms. Explain why junctional escape generally narrower, ventricular escape wider, rate differs, morphology depends on focus location. **"This should be one of the flagship simulator demonstrations."**

**16. VENTRICULAR ARRHYTHMIAS** — dedicated modules: PVCs; NSVT; monomorphic VT; polymorphic VT; torsades; VF; idiopathic VT; VT in structural heart disease. Teach re-entry; triggered activity; abnormal automaticity; scar-mediated VT; Purkinje-related mechanisms. **For every ventricular rhythm: mechanism → activation sequence → QRS morphology → hemodynamics → treatment.** Contemporary VA guidance.

**17. ISCHEMIA/INFARCTION** — dedicated interactive myocardial injury simulator. Selection of LAD; RCA; LCx; inferior; anterior; septal; lateral; posterior; RV territories. Show normal myocardium; ischemia; injury; infarction. Explain myocardial injury → altered transmembrane voltage → injury currents/vectors → lead-specific ST changes. Simulate hyperacute T; ST elevation; reciprocal ST depression; T-wave inversion; pathological Q waves.

**18. HYPERTROPHY** — LVH; RVH; atrial enlargement. Explain why increased mass changes QRS amplitude, axis, R-wave progression, repolarization, ST-T morphology. **"Do not teach voltage criteria without explaining the physiology behind them."**

**19. ELECTROLYTES** — hyperK; hypoK; hyperCa; hypoCa; magnesium. "Allow the user to progressively alter electrolyte concentration. Show the ECG changing continuously. Explain the cellular electrophysiology behind each change." HyperK: K gradient changes → RMP changes → Na-channel availability/conduction changes → T/QRS changes → severe conduction disturbance.

**20. DRUG/TOXICOLOGY** — β-blockers; CCBs; digoxin; Na-channel blockers; TCAs; antiarrhythmics; QT-prolonging meds. Mechanism → ECG (e.g. Na-channel blockade ↓ fast Na current → slowed phase 0 → slower ventricular conduction → wider QRS; QT drugs → altered repolarization → prolonged QT → EAD/torsades risk in susceptible settings).

**21. REPOLARIZATION DISORDERS** — long QT; short QT; early repolarization; Brugada pattern/syndrome; torsades. Explain AP → repolarization → transmural/vector differences → T/ST morphology. Current VA/electrical-disease guidance.

**22. PERICARDITIS/MYOCARDITIS** — acute pericarditis; myocarditis; myopericarditis; explain why ST elevation, PR depression, diffuse distribution, reciprocal changes where applicable.

**23. PULMONARY/RIGHT HEART** — PE; acute RV strain; pulmonary hypertension; COPD. Teach which findings are common, nonspecific, merely associations. **"Do not overstate the diagnostic value of classic patterns such as S1Q3T3."**

**24. PACEMAKER ECGS** — atrial pacing; ventricular pacing; AV sequential; appropriate sensing/capture; failure to capture; failure to sense; failure to pace. Explain pacing spikes and subsequent activation; why V pacing gives wide QRS.

**25. AXIS LAB** — full interactive; manipulate the cardiac vector through 360°; display simultaneously cardiac vector, limb-lead orientation, leads I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, resulting QRS polarity; simulate normal, LAD, RAD, extreme axis; demonstrate causes of axis changes.

**26. P-WAVE LAB** — manipulate atrial mass; R vs L atrial activation; atrial conduction velocity; interatrial conduction; ectopic atrial origin. Generate normal P; RAE; LAE; ectopic atrial rhythm; multifocal atrial activity; flutter; AF.

**27. QRS LAB** — manipulate ventricular mass; activation sequence; conduction velocity; axis; infarcted myocardium; AP; ventricular ectopic focus. Generate normal QRS; LVH; RVH; RBBB; LBBB; fascicular block; ventricular ectopy; ventricular rhythm; pre-excitation.

**28. ST/T/QT LAB** — manipulate ischemia; injury; repolarization velocity; calcium; potassium; autonomic effects; drugs; ventricular hypertrophy. Show ST, T polarity, T amplitude, QT, QTc. Explain cellular basis.

**29. "CHANGE ONE VARIABLE" MODE** — change exactly one physiological variable (e.g. increase AV nodal refractory period; move the ventricular pacemaker from apex to septum; increase K; block the right bundle; add an AP; increase LV mass; shift mean QRS axis 30° left). Show **Before → change → after** and explain exactly what changed.

**30. "BUILD YOUR OWN ARRHYTHMIA"** — select Pacemaker (sinus; atrial; junctional; ventricular); Conduction (normal; slowed; blocked; alternate pathway); Mechanism (automaticity; triggered; re-entry); Rates (atrial; ventricular); Refractoriness (atrial; AV node; ventricular). Generate rhythm + ECG; learners discover why AVNRT, AVRT, flutter, AF, VT, escape rhythms differ.

**31. MANAGEMENT MODULE** — every clinically important rhythm, at minimum: sinus brady; AV block; AF; flutter; AVNRT; AVRT; focal AT; MAT; VT; torsades; VF; WPW/pre-excitation; pre-excited AF; STEMI/ACS; electrolyte-related arrhythmia. For each: 1 Recognition; 2 Mechanism; 3 Stable vs unstable; 4 Immediate management; 5 Definitive management; 6 Important contraindications; 7 Why each treatment works; 8 Why certain treatments do not work.

**32. ADENOSINE PHYSIOLOGY DEMONSTRATOR** — learner selects AVNRT; AVRT; sinus tach; AF; flutter; 2nd-degree AVB; CHB; VT; clicks "Give adenosine"; simulator shows what happens (AVNRT: AV nodal blockade → interrupts circuit → termination; appropriate AVRT: can interrupt; sinus tach: does not eliminate sinus drive; AV block: further suppression doesn't correct conduction disease; CHB: ventricles dissociated so blockade can't restore conduction). "The educational explanation must explicitly distinguish the mechanism of the rhythm from the site of action of the medication."

**33. ACLS ECG CHEAT SHEET** — current AHA algorithms. Cardiac arrest: VF; pVT; PEA; asystole. Tachycardia with pulse: regular narrow; irregular narrow; regular wide; irregular wide. Bradycardia: sinus brady; AV block; symptomatic; unstable. Include recognition; immediate action; medications; pacing; cardioversion; defibrillation. "Use the current AHA ACLS algorithms rather than reproducing old ACLS protocols." 2025 tachycardia algorithm distinguishes narrow/wide and limits adenosine in WCT to regular monomorphic rhythms; 2025 bradycardia algorithm includes atropine, pacing, dopamine/epinephrine infusion, and consideration of transvenous pacing.

**34. ECG DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS ENGINE** — feature-based. Regular narrow-complex tachycardia: sinus tach; AVNRT; AVRT; flutter with fixed conduction; focal AT; discriminating features: P waves; RP; onset/termination; rate; regularity; vagal response; adenosine response. WCT: VT; SVT with aberrancy; antidromic AVRT; pre-excited AF; metabolic/toxicological conduction disturbance. **"Do not teach 'wide complex = VT' as an absolute rule."**

**35. ECG CASE GENERATOR** — realistic cases: age; symptoms; history; medications; vitals; ECG; optional labs. Learner determines 1 rate; 2 rhythm; 3 axis; 4 P waves; 5 PR; 6 QRS; 7 ST/T; 8 QT; 9 diagnosis; 10 mechanism; 11 management. Then complete explanation.

**36. PROGRESSIVE DIFFICULTY** — L1 recognition (sinus, AF, flutter, AV block, VT, VF); L2 interpretation (axis, intervals, morphology, conduction); L3 mechanistic ("Why does this ECG look like this?"); L4 electrophysiology (what circuit/abnormality generates it); L5 clinical management (what should be done and why).

**37. PATIENT VARIABILITY** — realistic variation in age; HR; autonomic tone; body habitus; cardiac position; chamber size; ventricular mass; conduction velocity; refractory periods; electrolytes; ischemic territory; infarct size; AP location; ectopic focus location. "Avoid deterministic statements when real-world ECG morphology is variable."

**38. ECG COMPARISON MODE** — normal vs abnormal; disease A vs B (AVNRT vs AVRT; AF vs flutter; VT vs SVT with aberrancy; RBBB vs LBBB; Mobitz I vs II; junctional vs ventricular escape; WPW vs normal; hyperK vs normal; pericarditis vs STEMI; LVH vs normal; RVH vs normal; paced vs intrinsic). Highlight the mechanistic reason for each difference.

**39. SEARCH** — by diagnosis; ECG finding; symptom; drug; mechanism; physiology; anatomical structure; treatment. "wide QRS" → ventricular rhythm, BBB, hyperK, Na-channel blockade, V pacing, pre-excitation, antidromic AVRT. "regular narrow tachycardia" → AVNRT, AVRT, flutter, focal AT, sinus tach. "adenosine" → AVNRT, AVRT, diagnostic AV block, AV nodal physiology, ACLS tachycardia algorithm, situations where adenosine should not be relied upon.

**40. CONTENT DATABASE** — every diagnosis a structured record: Definition; Epidemiology; Electrophysiological mechanism; ECG findings; Why ECG looks this way; Differential; Clinical presentation; Causes; Complications; Acute management; Long-term management; Contraindications/cautions; Physiology animation; Interactive ECG; Example ECGs; Practice cases; References.

**41. COVERAGE REQUIREMENT** — not complete merely with AF, WPW, Brugada, VT. Minimum: Rhythm (sinus rhythms; PAC/PVC; AT; MAT; flutter; AF; AVNRT; AVRT; junctional; ventricular escape; VT; torsades; VF); Conduction (1st-degree; Mobitz I; Mobitz II; high-grade; CHB; RBBB; LBBB; fascicular; bifascicular; nonspecific IVCD); Morphology (axis; atrial enlargement; ventricular hypertrophy; poor R-wave progression; Q waves; ST elevation; ST depression; T-wave abnormalities; QT abnormalities); Disease (ischemia/infarction; pericarditis; myocarditis; PE/right-heart strain; electrolytes; drug toxicity; pacing; WPW; Brugada; long QT; short QT; early repolarization); Emergency/ACLS (VF; pVT; PEA; asystole; unstable tachy; unstable brady; high-grade AV block).

**42. DO NOT OVERFOCUS ON RARE SYNDROMES** — depth ≈ clinical frequency × clinical importance × conceptual importance. Substantial coverage for AF, flutter, AVNRT, AVRT, SVT, PVCs, VT, AV block, BBB, ischemia, electrolytes, LVH, sinus abnormalities. Dedicated modules also for Brugada, long QT, pre-excited AF, torsades. Rare entities must not displace the core curriculum.

**43. THE APP'S DEFINING FEATURE** — learner should think **"I can predict what this ECG should look like because I understand the physiology."** Not "I memorized that this disease causes this ECG." Ultimate learning loop: **Change physiology → predict ECG → observe ECG → explain ECG → diagnose rhythm → choose treatment → understand why treatment works.** "That loop should be the organizing principle of the entire application."
